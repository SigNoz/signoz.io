If you use another logging platform, moving your logs to SigNoz Cloud can lower costs without reducing the volume you collect. Savings depend on your workload, retention, features and contract. To reduce volume further, remove unwanted events before ingestion and check that failed requests still have the logs you need.

The [SigNoz–Datadog cost comparison](https://signoz.io/blog/cost-effective-datadog-alternative/) illustrates the first option. It models lower log costs at the same monthly volume and 15-day retention, with no separate log indexing fee in SigNoz Cloud. Compare [current pricing](https://signoz.io/pricing/) for your own workload and required features.

If you already use OpenTelemetry, you can keep that instrumentation and change the export destination and credentials. The [migration guide](https://signoz.io/docs/migration/migrate-from-opentelemetry-to-signoz/) covers the configuration changes and checks that data arrives.

## Choose the events you can remove

For example, take a checkout service called `checkout-api` that writes a log for every `/healthz` check. If your team does not need a record of each successful check, you can filter those logs out. In this example, we removed only the successful health-check logs. We kept failed health checks and every log for a failed payment, including the DEBUG and INFO messages that explained the request.

Log level alone does not tell you whether a record is useful. A DEBUG message can name the payment gateway, while INFO messages can show the request start and failed response. Keep the error bodies, request identifiers and surrounding records that your team uses to investigate failures.

## Filter before ingestion

An OpenTelemetry Collector receives logs and forwards them to a backend. Its <a href="https://opentelemetry.io/docs/collector/transforming-telemetry/#basic-filtering" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">filter processor</a> can remove a chosen event before export to SigNoz or another backend. The billing effect depends on the destination's pricing.

For the checkout example, our rule matched the service, successful health-check event, route, status, severity and message together. That kept failed health checks outside the match. The [log filtering guide](https://signoz.io/docs/logs-management/guides/drop-logs/) explains how to configure and enable a filter for your pipeline.

In SigNoz Cloud, remove unwanted records before ingestion to reduce billed log volume. [Logs Pipelines in the SigNoz UI run after ingestion](https://signoz.io/docs/cost-control/optimize-logs/), so their parsing and enrichment do not reduce that volume.

## Check that the incident still makes sense

When we tested this example, removing 20 successful health-check logs reduced a 32-record sample to 12 records. All six failed-payment records remained, with their error bodies and request identifiers intact.

The DEBUG message still names the payment gateway involved in the failed request. With that context intact, measured ingestion fell from 10,158 to 3,798 bytes. These results apply to this sample and do not predict your production bill.

For your service, compare a known failed request before and after filtering, and check that failed health checks still arrive. Use [Cost Meter](https://signoz.io/docs/cost-meter/overview/) to compare ingested bytes under comparable traffic, because record counts alone can mislead. Meter data updates hourly. Keep the previous configuration so you can resume collection if needed; removing a filter cannot recover logs already dropped.
