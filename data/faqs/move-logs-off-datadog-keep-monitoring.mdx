You can move a service’s logs to SigNoz Cloud while keeping metrics and application performance monitoring (APM) in Datadog or other tools. By changing only the log collection route, you can leave the components that collect metrics and traces in place.

## Start with one service

For example, take a checkout service that records payment results. When a payment times out, the checkout service logs the timeout and failed response. You want to search those logs in SigNoz Cloud while an inventory service keeps its current log destination.

You can first send copies of checkout’s logs to SigNoz Cloud while the existing destination continues to receive both services. This gives you time to compare the logs before switching checkout over. If the source already uses an OpenTelemetry Collector, its log pipeline can route the copies. Sending logs to both destinations can incur charges at both.

The [Datadog logs migration guide](https://signoz.io/docs/migration/migrate-from-datadog/logs/) covers collection options for different sources. If changing a shared Collector would interrupt metrics or traces, a separate log Collector can help keep those routes in place.

## Check the payment trail before you switch

In SigNoz Cloud, find a known failed payment in **Logs Explorer**. Search its request ID to check that the timeout and failed response both arrive. A request ID separates that payment from other requests to the service. In our sample, the search returned the two failure records shown below.

Compare the logs at both destinations over the same time range, including counts, timestamps and the fields your searches use. Before switching checkout’s route, recreate its needed log searches and alerts in SigNoz Cloud and test that alerts and notifications work. Datadog log monitors will no longer see new checkout logs once those logs stop arriving there. The [alert migration guide](https://signoz.io/docs/migration/migrate-from-datadog/alerts/) explains how to map and test alerts.

Once those checks pass, you can remove checkout’s old log route while leaving inventory’s route and metric and trace monitoring in place. Keep the previous configuration so you can restore the old route if needed, and check that new checkout logs reach the original destination after restoration.

In our example, a second Collector stood in for the original logging backend. We switched checkout's logs to SigNoz Cloud, then restored the old route. After restoration, new checkout logs reached the original Collector and no longer appeared in SigNoz Cloud.

Changing the route applies to new logs; it does not move your historical Datadog logs. Those logs remain subject to their existing retention settings. Saved searches, parsing rules, dashboards and alerts also need a separate migration plan, and restoring the route cannot recover logs that were never collected.

## Bring logs, metrics and traces together

You can start with logs and later bring checkout's metrics and traces into SigNoz Cloud to investigate in one place. When errors rise, compare traffic and error trends in the metrics, open a failed payment's trace to inspect its recorded service calls, and read the logs for the error details.

To open a matching trace from a log, the log needs its trace ID and the trace must still be available in SigNoz Cloud. The [logs and traces correlation guide](https://signoz.io/docs/traces-management/guides/correlate-traces-and-logs/) covers the setup.

Moving more telemetry from Datadog to SigNoz Cloud can also lower your overall costs. The [cost comparison](https://signoz.io/blog/cost-effective-datadog-alternative/) models one workload; compare [current pricing](https://signoz.io/pricing/) with your own volume, retention, required features and contract.
