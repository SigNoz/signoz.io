You can move a service’s logs to SigNoz Cloud while keeping metrics and application performance monitoring (APM) in Datadog or other tools. Change the log collection route and keep the components that collect your metrics and traces in place.

## Start with one service

For example, take a checkout service that records payment results. When a payment times out, the checkout service logs the timeout and failed response. You want to search those logs in SigNoz Cloud while an inventory service keeps its current log destination.

An OpenTelemetry Collector receives logs and forwards them to a backend. If your chosen source already uses a Collector, its logs pipeline can send copies to both destinations during a short comparison. A service filter selects the checkout logs for SigNoz Cloud while the current destination continues to receive both services. Sending copies to two destinations can incur charges at both.

Keep the change limited to logs. Use a separate log Collector when changing a shared Collector would interrupt collection of metrics and traces. The [Datadog logs migration guide](https://signoz.io/docs/migration/migrate-from-datadog/logs/) covers collection options for your source.

## Check the payment trail before you switch

In SigNoz Cloud, find a known failed payment in **Logs Explorer**. Search its request ID to check that the timeout and failed response both arrive. A request ID separates that payment from other requests to the service. In our sample, the search returned the two failure records shown below.

Compare the records at both destinations over the same fixed time range. Check counts, original timestamps, severity, service name and the fields your searches use. Recreate the required log searches and alerts, then check that they identify the intended failures before you rely on them. The [alert migration guide](https://signoz.io/docs/migration/migrate-from-datadog/alerts/) explains how to map Datadog monitors to SigNoz alerts.

Once those checks pass, you can remove checkout’s old log route while leaving inventory’s route and existing monitoring in place. Keep the previous configuration so you can restore checkout’s old route if needed. After restoring it, check that new checkout logs reach the original destination.

In our example, a second Collector stood in for the original logging backend. We switched checkout's logs to SigNoz Cloud, then restored the old route. After restoration, new checkout logs reached the original Collector and no longer appeared in SigNoz Cloud.

Changing the log route applies to new logs. Your historical Datadog logs stay there, and saved searches, parsing rules, dashboards and alerts need a separate migration plan. Restoring the route cannot recover logs that were never collected.

## Bring logs, metrics and traces together

You can start with logs and later bring checkout's metrics and traces into SigNoz Cloud to investigate in one place. When errors rise, compare traffic and error trends in the metrics, open a failed payment's trace to inspect its recorded service calls, and read the logs for the error details.

To open a matching trace from a log, the log needs its trace ID and the trace must still be available in SigNoz Cloud. The [logs and traces correlation guide](https://signoz.io/docs/traces-management/guides/correlate-traces-and-logs/) covers the setup.

Moving more telemetry from Datadog to SigNoz Cloud can also lower your overall costs. The [cost comparison](https://signoz.io/blog/cost-effective-datadog-alternative/) models one workload; compare [current pricing](https://signoz.io/pricing/) with your own volume, retention, required features and contract.
