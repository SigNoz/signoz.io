You can send a service’s logs to SigNoz Cloud while keeping its metrics and application performance monitoring (APM) in Datadog or other tools. The change is limited to where the logs go, so the components that collect metrics and traces can stay in place.

## Start with one service

For example, take a checkout service that records payment results. When a payment times out, checkout logs the timeout and failed response. You want to search those logs in SigNoz Cloud while an inventory service keeps its current log destination. In our example, an OpenTelemetry Collector receives the logs in place of Datadog. A Collector receives and forwards telemetry such as logs.

You can first send copies of checkout’s logs to SigNoz Cloud while the existing destination continues to receive logs from both services. This gives you time to compare them before switching checkout over. If the source already uses a Collector, it can route those copies. Sending logs to both destinations can incur charges at both.

The [Datadog logs migration guide](https://signoz.io/docs/migration/migrate-from-datadog/logs/) covers collection options for different sources. If the same Collector also handles metrics and traces, a separate log Collector can help keep their collection in place while you change the log route.

## Check the payment trail before you switch

To check a payment in SigNoz Cloud, search for its request ID in **Logs Explorer**. The ID helps distinguish that payment’s logs from those of other requests. In our example, the search returned both the timeout and failed response for one payment, as shown below.

That payment is one useful check. To assess the broader stream, compare logs at both destinations over the same time range, including counts, timestamps and the fields your searches use. Datadog log monitors will no longer see new checkout logs once those logs stop arriving there, so recreate the searches and alerts you need in SigNoz Cloud and test the alerts and notifications before switching. The [alert migration guide](https://signoz.io/docs/migration/migrate-from-datadog/alerts/) explains how to map and test them.

Once those checks pass, you can remove checkout’s old log route while leaving inventory’s route and metric and trace monitoring in place. Keep the previous configuration in case you need to restore it, and check that new checkout logs reach the original destination after restoration. In our example, we switched checkout’s logs to SigNoz Cloud and then restored the route to the Collector acting as the existing destination. New checkout logs reached that Collector and no longer appeared in SigNoz Cloud.

The route change affects new logs; it does not move historical Datadog logs, which remain subject to their existing retention settings. Saved searches, parsing rules, dashboards and alerts need a separate migration plan. Restoring the route also cannot recover logs that were never collected.

## Bring logs, metrics and traces together

Starting with logs lets you make the move gradually. If you later bring checkout’s metrics and traces into SigNoz Cloud, you can investigate a payment failure in one place. Metrics show traffic and error trends, the failed payment’s trace shows its recorded service calls, and its logs provide error details.

A trace ID links a log to the corresponding request trace. To open that trace from a log, the log needs its trace ID and the trace must still be available in SigNoz Cloud. The [logs and traces correlation guide](https://signoz.io/docs/traces-management/guides/correlate-traces-and-logs/) covers the setup.

If you are considering moving more telemetry, compare the cost for your workload as well. Moving more telemetry from Datadog to SigNoz Cloud can lower your overall costs, but the result depends on your volume, retention, required features and contract. The [cost comparison](https://signoz.io/blog/cost-effective-datadog-alternative/) models one workload, and [current pricing](https://signoz.io/pricing/) can help with your own estimate.
