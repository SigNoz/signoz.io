The **Trace Details** interface in SigNoz offers a powerful and intuitive interface to visualize and analyze the journey of a request through your system. By displaying detailed trace information, it enables you to quickly diagnose performance issues, pinpoint errors, and understand the intricate flow of operations in your systems. It is engineered to handle massive trace data supporting more than a **million spans per trace** making it an ideal solution for high-volume environments.

<Admonition type="info">
The Trace Details interface described below is now the default experience for every trace link in SigNoz. Opening a trace from the [Trace Explorer](https://signoz.io/docs/userguide/traces/), an alert, or a deep link lands you directly on the synchronized flamegraph and waterfall view — no separate URL or opt-in step is required.
</Admonition>

The Trace Details Page enables you to:

- **Visualize the Entire Trace:** Render large traces seamlessly using synchronized flame graphs and waterfall charts.
- **Drill Down Into Details:** Inspect individual spans to view metadata, span percentile, context, attributes and events associated with each span.
- **Efficient Navigation and Search:** Use the dynamic search bar, quick filters, and highlight errors to quickly jump to specific spans or error events.
- **Responsive and Scalable:** Handle traces from your architectures (monolithic or microservices) without performance lag, ensuring smooth and real-time analysis.

## Key Concepts

- **Trace:** A trace represents the complete path a request takes through your system, helping you understand its flow across various components and services.
- **Span:** Spans are the building blocks of a trace, representing individual units of work performed by an application or service during the request's lifecycle. Key components of a span include:
  - **Span Context:** Metadata that links spans across services, enabling distributed tracing and hierarchical structuring.
  - **Span Attributes:** Key-value pairs that store additional metadata about the operation performed by the span.
  - **Span Links:** References that establish causal relationships between spans, useful for correlating asynchronous operations or background tasks with the main trace.
  - **Span Events:** Structured log messages that record important events at specific timestamps during the span's execution.

## Opening a Trace

Click any trace from the [Trace Explorer](https://signoz.io/docs/userguide/traces/), an [APM service page](https://signoz.io/docs/userguide/metrics/), or a trace link from an alert, dashboard, or related signal to open the Trace Details page at `/trace/<traceId>`. You will see two synchronized views:

- **Flame Graph** — a canvas-based, hierarchical view of the trace showing the nested relationships between spans. The header shows the total span count and error count. It renders **100,000+ spans** at once.
- **Waterfall Chart** — a detailed timeline view showing the sequence and duration of each span. It supports **10,000+ spans** with virtual scrolling for smooth navigation.

A draggable divider separates the flamegraph and waterfall — drag it to resize either section. Both views are also collapsible: click the section header to expand or collapse them and maximize screen space.

## Navigating the Flame Graph

The flame graph is canvas-rendered and supports interactive navigation:

- **Zoom** — use the mouse wheel over the flamegraph to zoom in and out along the time axis.
- **Pan** — click and drag the flamegraph to scroll horizontally through long traces.
- **Crosshair and time badge** — a vertical crosshair follows the cursor across the flamegraph, and a small badge shows the exact timestamp at the cursor position.
- **Hover tooltip** — hover any span bar to see a tooltip with the span name, status, relative start time, and duration.
- **Event markers** — span events appear as small markers on the corresponding span bar. Hover a marker to see a per-event tooltip with the event name and timestamp.

## Inspecting a Span

Click a span in either the flame graph or the waterfall view to open the **Span Details** panel. The panel can be displayed as a floating window, docked to the bottom of the page, or pinned as a right-side drawer — see [Adjusting the Interface](#adjusting-the-interface) for switching modes.

The panel header shows:
- **Span name** and **service name**
- **Percentile ranking** (e.g., p52, p100) and duration
- **Percentage of total execution time**
- **Timestamp** and **linked spans count**
- **Service**, **Status Code**, **Trace ID**, and **Span Kind**
- A **copy link** action that generates a shareable deep-link URL containing the trace and span IDs (see [Sharing a Span](#sharing-a-span))

The details are organized into tabs:

- **Overview** — span attributes in a structured view
- **Events** — timestamped log messages and span events recorded during the span
- **Logs** — correlated log lines from the span's service and time window
- **Metrics** — infrastructure metrics (CPU, memory) for the host or pod

### Attributes

The Overview tab displays all resource and span attributes. You can toggle between **Pretty** and raw views:

- **Pretty view** — displays attributes in their original, nested JSON structure as ingested, making it easier to understand complex attribute hierarchies.

Hover over any attribute value to see quick action icons:

- **Pin Attribute** — pin the attribute to the top of the list for easy reference
- **Filter for Value** — add the attribute as an `IN` filter to the current query
- **Filter out Value** — add the attribute as a `NOT IN` filter to exclude matching spans
- **Group By Attribute** — add the attribute to the Group By clause
- **Copy Field Name** — copy the attribute key to your clipboard
- **Copy Field Value** — copy the attribute value to your clipboard

### Synchronized Navigation

The flame graph and waterfall views remain synchronized. Clicking a span in one view automatically highlights it in the other and opens the Span Details panel, ensuring you always see where a span fits into the overall trace timeline.

- **Clicking a span in the waterfall** updates the Span Details panel in place without refetching the trace, even when the selected span sits at the top or bottom of the visible window.
- **Clicking a node in the flamegraph** automatically scrolls the waterfall to center the matching span.
- **Browser back/forward and the filter prev/next arrows** move between spans you have already visited and keep the waterfall scrolled to the selected span.

### Linked Spans

If a span has [non-parent references](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/concepts/signals/traces/#span-links) — for example, spans correlated across asynchronous workflows or queues — the Span Details panel header shows a **linked spans** count and a section listing each reference. Click any linked span entry to jump directly to that span in the waterfall; the flame graph and Span Details panel update in sync.

### Sharing a Span

The Span Details panel header includes a **copy link** action next to the Trace ID. Click it to copy a deep-link URL that points to this trace and span. A confirmation toast appears when the URL is copied. Pasting the link opens the same span — selected, scrolled into view, and with the Span Details panel open in the same tab.

### Add to Funnel

Click **Add to Funnel** on a span to open the Add to Funnel modal. From there you can add the span to an existing [trace funnel](https://signoz.io/docs/trace-funnels/overview/) or create a new one. The action is available from the per-span controls in the waterfall and from the Span Details panel.

## Options Menu

Click the **three-dot menu** (⋯) in the top bar to access additional trace options:

- **Hide trace details** — collapses the sub-header row that displays the service name, operation, duration, timestamp, and status code
- **Preview fields** — opens a panel where you can select span attributes to display inline in the waterfall rows, so you can see key values without opening the Span Details panel
- **Colour by** — choose how spans are colored in the flame graph and waterfall (e.g., by `service.name` or `host.name`)

The Colour by submenu lets you customize how spans are colored in the flame graph and waterfall views. The following attributes are available:

- `service.name`
- `service.namespace`
- `host.name`
- `k8s.node.name`

## Waterfall Hover Tooltip

Hover over any span row in the waterfall to see a tooltip with key details:

- **Span name** — the operation name
- **Status** — whether the span completed successfully or with an error
- **Start** — the span's start time relative to the trace start
- **Duration** — how long the span took

You can add more fields to the tooltip by selecting attributes through the **Options menu (⋯) → Preview fields**. Any attribute you add there (up to 10) will also appear in the hover tooltip alongside the default fields.

## Filtering and Analysis

The Trace Details page provides multiple ways to filter and analyze spans within a trace.

### Highlight Errors

Toggle **Highlight Errors** in the top-right corner to instantly focus on error spans. When enabled:

- A `has_error = true` filter is applied automatically
- Non-error spans are **dimmed**, making red error spans stand out
- A **counter** (e.g., 1 / 3) appears, letting you navigate between error spans using the up/down arrows

<Figure
  src="/img/docs/apm-and-distributed-tracing/trace-details-highlight-errors.webp"
  alt="Highlight Errors toggle dimming non-error spans"
  caption="Highlight Errors — non-error spans are dimmed, error spans stand out with navigation counter"
  className="box-shadowed-image"
/>

### Quick Filters

Quick filter buttons at the top of the page provide one-click filtering by span kind:

- **All** — show all spans
- **Database** — filter to database operations
- **Functions** — filter to function calls
- **HTTP** — filter to HTTP spans
- **Jobs** — filter to job/task spans
- **LLM** — filter to LLM-related spans

These filters can be combined with the Highlight Errors toggle for targeted analysis.

### Query Builder

The integrated query builder allows you to write custom filter queries directly in the filter bar. For example:

- `http.status_code >= 500 AND service.name = 'frontend'` — find server errors in a specific service
- `http.method exists` — filter to HTTP spans only

## Adjusting the Interface

The Span Details panel opens by default as a **docked drawer on the right**. Use the dock toggles in the panel header to switch between three layout modes:

- **Right-side drawer** (default) — panel pinned to the right, ideal for viewing attributes alongside the trace.
- **Bottom dock** — panel docked at the bottom of the page, maximizing the horizontal trace view.
- **Floating dialog** — panel detaches into a free-floating window that can be **dragged anywhere** on screen and **resized in all directions** for full control over your workspace.

You can also close the panel entirely and reopen it by clicking any span in the flame graph or waterfall.

### Logs

Click the **Logs** tab to see log lines correlated to the span's time window and service. The panel shows log entries with timestamps and message bodies, and includes a link to open the same context logs in the [Logs Explorer](https://signoz.io/docs/userguide/logs/) for deeper analysis.

### Metrics

Click the **Metrics** tab to see infrastructure metrics (CPU usage, memory usage) for the host or pod that served the selected span. Use the **Node** and **Pod** tabs to switch between host-level and pod-level views.

<Admonition type="info">
Pod and node metrics require Kubernetes resource attributes on the span (for example, `k8s.pod.name`, `k8s.node.name`). The Metrics tab only displays infrastructure data when these attributes are present on the selected span.

## Legacy View

The Trace Details interface described above is the default for all trace links in SigNoz. If you need the previous trace details interface, click the **Legacy View** (Old View) button in the top-right corner of the Trace Details header to switch back — the legacy UI is served from the `/trace-old/<traceId>` route and is reachable only through this toggle or a direct URL. SigNoz remembers your preference for future visits.
