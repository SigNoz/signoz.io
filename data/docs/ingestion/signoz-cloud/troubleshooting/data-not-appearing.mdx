## Overview

It is frustrating to finish instrumenting your application and then stare at an empty dashboard. The good news: when telemetry does not show up in SigNoz, it is almost always getting lost at one of three well-defined points on the path from your app to SigNoz, and you can usually find which one in a few minutes.

This guide walks those three points in order. You do not have to read all of it. Start with the [interactive troubleshooter](#find-your-problem) below, or follow the steps manually. Each step tells you what to check, what a healthy result looks like, and where to go next based on what you find.

1. [Is your application generating telemetry?](#step-1-is-your-application-generating-telemetry) Confirm the data exists before worrying about where it goes.
2. [Is your telemetry being exported?](#step-2-is-your-telemetry-being-exported) Enable diagnostic logging and read the exporter errors.
3. [Is your telemetry reaching SigNoz?](#step-3-is-your-telemetry-reaching-signoz) Verify connectivity and the ingestion endpoint or Collector.

<Admonition type="tip">
Most "no data" issues are a wrong ingestion endpoint, a wrong region, or an incorrect ingestion key. If you already know your instrumentation works, skip straight to [Errors you might see](#errors-you-might-see).
</Admonition>

## Signal-specific notes

Each signal reaches SigNoz a little differently. Expand your telemetry type for its common sources and the checks specific to it.

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### Traces
</ToggleHeading>

Traces reach SigNoz from the OpenTelemetry SDK's OTLP exporter, either directly or through a Collector.

- **Common sources:** OpenTelemetry SDK; Jaeger/Zipkin clients. Spans from **Jaeger or Zipkin clients** must pass through a Collector with the matching receiver, which converts them to OTLP.
- If some spans are missing or you see gaps in a trace, the instrumentation is likely producing data but some spans are dropped or uninstrumented. See [Traces Management FAQs](https://signoz.io/docs/traces-management/troubleshooting/faqs/).
- SigNoz builds the **Services** list in APM by grouping incoming spans on their `service.name` resource attribute. If your traces are arriving but a service is missing from the Services tab, it usually means `service.name` is unset or not unique. Set a unique `service.name` on that service.

</details>

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### Logs
</ToggleHeading>

Logs reach SigNoz from the OpenTelemetry SDK or log appenders, from files on disk read by the Collector's `filelog` receiver, or from log shippers, over OTLP, directly or through a Collector.

**Common sources:** OpenTelemetry SDK / log appenders; files on disk; log shippers (FluentBit, Fluentd, Logstash, Vector, Telegraf). Identify your path first:

- **Log shippers or the Collector.** Check that receivers and exporters are wired into the pipeline correctly, that the exporter endpoint and ingestion key are set, and read the Collector or shipper logs for errors.
- **Files on disk.** The Collector's `filelog` receiver reads log files (the standard path for Kubernetes and Docker). Confirm the receiver's include paths match your log file locations.
- **SDK or log-appender bridge.** Run the console-exporter check in [Step 1](#step-1-is-your-application-generating-telemetry) to confirm the SDK emits logs, then follow Steps 2 and 3.

To correlate logs with traces, make sure each log line carries `trace_id` and `span_id` and parse them with the [trace parser pipeline](https://signoz.io/docs/logs-pipelines/guides/trace/). More in [Logs Management FAQs](https://signoz.io/docs/logs-management/troubleshooting/faqs/).

</details>

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### Metrics
</ToggleHeading>

Metrics reach SigNoz from the OpenTelemetry SDK, or from Prometheus, StatsD, and host or Kubernetes sources collected by the Collector's receivers, exported as OTLP.

- **OpenTelemetry SDK metrics** export OTLP directly or through a Collector.
- **Prometheus metrics** are collected by the Collector's `prometheus` receiver, which scrapes your `/metrics` endpoints.
- **StatsD** metrics are received by the Collector's `statsd` receiver (the app pushes UDP packets to the listener), then exported as OTLP.
- **Host and Kubernetes metrics** come from the `hostmetrics` receiver and the [K8s-Infra chart](https://signoz.io/docs/opentelemetry-collection-agents/k8s/k8s-infra/overview/).
- For any Collector receiver, confirm receivers and exporters are wired into the pipeline, the exporter endpoint and key are set, and check the Collector logs for errors.
- If a specific metric is missing, a common cause is a temporality mismatch (delta vs cumulative). See [Metrics Management FAQs](https://signoz.io/docs/metrics-management/troubleshooting/faqs/) and [temporality of metrics](https://signoz.io/docs/metrics-management/types-and-aggregation/#temporality-of-metrics).

</details>

## Step 1: Is your application generating telemetry?

Before checking the network, confirm the telemetry actually exists. The most reliable way is to route the data to your console so you can see it printed locally.

### Using the console exporter

How you do this depends on your language, because not every SDK reads the exporter environment variables.

<Tabs entityName="sdk">
<TabItem value="agents" label="Java, Node.js, Python, .NET, Ruby" default>

These auto-instrumentation agents read the OpenTelemetry exporter environment variables. Set the exporter to `console` (the current spec value, not the deprecated `logging`) and telemetry prints to stdout:

```bash
export OTEL_TRACES_EXPORTER=console
export OTEL_METRICS_EXPORTER=console
export OTEL_LOGS_EXPORTER=console
```

To keep exporting to SigNoz **and** print to the console at the same time, use a comma-separated list, for example `OTEL_TRACES_EXPORTER=otlp,console`.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="php" label="PHP">

PHP reads the same environment variables, but you must also enable autoloading. Set the exporter to `console` and turn on `OTEL_PHP_AUTOLOAD_ENABLED`:

```bash
export OTEL_PHP_AUTOLOAD_ENABLED=true
export OTEL_TRACES_EXPORTER=console
export OTEL_METRICS_EXPORTER=console
export OTEL_LOGS_EXPORTER=console
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="go" label="Go">

The Go SDK has no auto-instrumentation agent and does not read `OTEL_*_EXPORTER`. Add a stdout exporter in code, either with the <a href="https://pkg.go.dev/go.opentelemetry.io/contrib/exporters/autoexport" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">`autoexport`</a> contrib module (which then honors `OTEL_TRACES_EXPORTER=console`) or by wiring `stdouttrace` / `stdoutmetric` / `stdoutlog` directly. See the [Go instrumentation guide](https://signoz.io/docs/instrumentation/opentelemetry-golang/).

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

Restart your application and exercise the code path you expect to produce data (send a request, run the job, emit a log).

- **You see spans, metrics, or logs printed in stdout.** Your instrumentation works. The data exists, so the problem is in export or connectivity. Continue to [Step 2](#step-2-is-your-telemetry-being-exported).
- **You see nothing printed.** Your application is not producing telemetry. This is an instrumentation problem, not a networking one. See [When no data is generated](#when-no-data-is-generated) below.

<Admonition type="tip">
For a Collector-based setup, do the equivalent check at the Collector using the [debug exporter](#verify-data-inside-the-collector) in Step 3. That tells you whether data is reaching the Collector at all.
</Admonition>

### When no data is generated

If nothing is printed to the console, the instrumentation is not active or is not supported for your setup. Check the following:

- **The instrumentation is actually initialized.** Auto-instrumentation must be loaded before your app code runs (for example, the Java agent via `-javaagent`, `opentelemetry-instrument` for Python, or requiring the SDK first in Node.js). Follow the setup steps in your language's [instrumentation guide](https://signoz.io/docs/instrumentation/overview/).
- **Your runtime version is supported.** Very old runtimes (for example, .NET Framework 4.5, Node.js 14, Python 3.1) may be outside the supported range for the OpenTelemetry SDK. Check the requirements in your language's guide and in the <a href="https://opentelemetry.io/docs/languages/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">OpenTelemetry language documentation</a>.
- **Your third-party library version is supported.** Auto-instrumentation hooks into specific versions of frameworks and clients. If a library is too new or too old, its spans may not be produced. See the supported libraries lists in the <a href="https://opentelemetry.io/docs/languages/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">OpenTelemetry docs</a>.
- **The code path is exercised.** Confirm the request or job you expect to trace actually ran while instrumentation was active.

## Step 2: Is your telemetry being exported?

If the data exists but is not reaching SigNoz, the exporter is either failing or misconfigured. Turn on the SDK's diagnostic logging to see the exporter's own error messages.

<Tabs entityName="language">
<TabItem value="nodejs" label="Node.js" default>

Set the log level to `debug`:

```bash
export OTEL_LOG_LEVEL=debug
```

The SDK prints exporter activity and any connection or authentication errors to the console.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="java" label="Java">

Enable the Java agent's debug logging:

```bash
export OTEL_JAVAAGENT_DEBUG=true
# or as a JVM flag: -Dotel.javaagent.debug=true
```

<Admonition type="tip">
Agent debug logs are verbose and go to stderr by default. To route them through your application's own slf4j/logback logging instead, set `OTEL_JAVAAGENT_LOGGING=application` (default is `simple`).
</Admonition>

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

For zero-code instrumentation, set the log level to `debug`:

```bash
export OTEL_PYTHON_LOG_LEVEL=debug
opentelemetry-instrument python your_app.py
```

For manual SDK setup, configure Python's standard `logging` module instead:

```python
import logging
logging.basicConfig(level=logging.DEBUG)
```

Either way, re-run your app and read the exporter output. See the <a href="https://opentelemetry.io/docs/zero-code/python/configuration/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">OpenTelemetry Python configuration docs</a> for more.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="go" label="Go">

The Go SDK has no debug env var. Export errors already print to stderr by default through the built-in error handler, with no setup required. For more detail, set a custom logger in code:

```go
import (
    "go.opentelemetry.io/otel"
    "github.com/go-logr/stdr"
    "log"
)

otel.SetLogger(stdr.New(log.Default()))
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="dotnet" label=".NET">

Set the log level to `debug` before starting the instrumented process:

```bash
export OTEL_LOG_LEVEL=debug
```

This applies to the zero-code auto-instrumentation agent. Logs are written to a default location; override it with `OTEL_DOTNET_AUTO_LOG_DIRECTORY` if needed.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="other" label="Other languages">

Enable the SDK's internal (self-diagnostic) logging as described in your language's <a href="https://opentelemetry.io/docs/languages/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">OpenTelemetry documentation</a>, then re-run your application and read the exporter output.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

With diagnostic logging on, restart the app and look at the output:

- **You see an export error.** The exporter is trying and failing. Match the error against [Errors you might see](#errors-you-might-see) to correct the endpoint, region, or key.
- **You see no errors and exports appear to succeed.** The data is leaving your application cleanly. Continue to [Step 3](#step-3-is-your-telemetry-reaching-signoz).

## Step 3: Is your telemetry reaching SigNoz?

How you verify this depends on your topology.

### If you export directly to SigNoz Cloud

Test that the ingestion endpoint is reachable from the machine running your application:

```bash
curl -v https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443
```

**Verify these values:**

- `<region>` is the region shown under **Settings → Ingestion** in the SigNoz UI. See the [ingestion overview](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/overview/).

If the connection fails, a firewall or network policy is blocking outbound access to the endpoint. If it connects but data still does not appear, the problem is the endpoint, region, or key, covered in [Errors you might see](#errors-you-might-see). For a full catalog of ingestion status codes (400, 401, 404, and so on) and their fixes, see the [Ingestion Troubleshooting guide](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/troubleshooting/troubleshooting/).

### If you use an OpenTelemetry Collector

There are two links to verify: application → Collector, and Collector → SigNoz.

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### Verify data inside the Collector
</ToggleHeading>

This `debug` exporter is a Collector pipeline component, different from the SDK's `OTEL_LOG_LEVEL=debug` in [Step 2](#step-2-is-your-telemetry-being-exported): Step 2 shows whether your *application* is exporting, while this shows what the *Collector* actually receives. Add it to the relevant pipeline in your existing `otel-collector-config.yaml` (do not replace the whole file):

```yaml:otel-collector-config.yaml
receivers:
  otlp:                                                # data coming IN from your app
    protocols:
      grpc:
        endpoint: 0.0.0.0:4317                         # OTLP/gRPC: your app sends here
      http:
        endpoint: 0.0.0.0:4318                         # OTLP/HTTP: your app sends here

exporters:
  debug:
    verbosity: detailed
  otlphttp:                                            # data going OUT to SigNoz
    # SigNoz Cloud endpoint: port 443, not 4317/4318
    endpoint: https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443
    headers:
      signoz-ingestion-key: <your-ingestion-key>

# The service section wires components into pipelines.
# REQUIRED: a receiver or exporter does nothing until it is listed here.
service:
  pipelines:
    traces:
      receivers: [otlp]
      processors: [batch]
      exporters: [debug, otlphttp]
    logs:
      receivers: [otlp]
      processors: [batch]
      exporters: [debug, otlphttp]
    metrics:
      receivers: [otlp]
      processors: [batch]
      exporters: [debug, otlphttp]
```

<Admonition type="warning">
Configuring a receiver or exporter is not enough on its own: the Collector runs a component only once it is wired into a pipeline under `service.pipelines`. If you define the `otlphttp` and `debug` exporters but forget to list them in the relevant pipeline's `exporters`, nothing is sent and no data reaches SigNoz. Always configure the `service` section too. See the [Collector configuration guide](https://signoz.io/docs/opentelemetry-collection-agents/opentelemetry-collector/configuration/).
</Admonition>

The Collector has two sides, and they use **different ports**. This is where most people mix things up:

- **Receivers** (`otlp`) are the ports the Collector *listens on* for data coming from your application: `4317` for OTLP/gRPC and `4318` for OTLP/HTTP. Point your app's `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` at these (for example `http://<collector-host>:4318`).
- **Exporters** (`otlphttp`) are where the Collector *sends* data onward. For SigNoz Cloud the endpoint is `https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443` (port `443`, **not** `4317`/`4318`); for self-hosted SigNoz it is `http://<signoz-host>:4318`.

So `443` only appears on the exporter side (Collector → SigNoz Cloud); `4317`/`4318` only appear on the receiver side (your app → Collector).

**Verify these values:**

- `<region>` and `<your-ingestion-key>` come from **Settings → Ingestion** in the SigNoz UI. See [ingestion keys](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/keys/).

Restart the Collector and check its logs.

- **You see data in the debug output but not in SigNoz.** The Collector receives data but cannot export it. The problem is the `otlphttp` exporter config (endpoint, region, or key). See [Errors you might see](#errors-you-might-see).
- **You see no data in the debug output.** Data is not reaching the Collector. Check that the application can connect to the Collector, below.

Reference: the <a href="https://github.com/open-telemetry/opentelemetry-collector/blob/main/exporter/debugexporter/README.md" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">debug exporter documentation</a> and the <a href="https://opentelemetry.io/docs/collector/troubleshooting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">Collector troubleshooting guide</a>.

</details>

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### Verify the application can reach the Collector
</ToggleHeading>

Confirm the application points at the Collector:

```bash
echo $OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT
# Expect http://localhost:4318 (OTLP/HTTP) or your Collector host and port
```

Confirm the Collector is listening on the OTLP ports:

```bash
netstat -tuln | grep -E '4317|4318'
```

Ports `4317` and `4318` should be in the `LISTEN` state.

For Kubernetes, verify the Collector Service has endpoints:

```bash
kubectl get svc otel-collector -n observability
kubectl get endpoints otel-collector -n observability
```

<Admonition type="warning">
Watch for port and protocol mismatches when talking to a **Collector or self-hosted SigNoz**: OTLP/gRPC uses port `4317` and OTLP/HTTP uses port `4318`. If your SDK is configured for HTTP but points at `4317` (or the reverse), exports fail silently or with connection errors. Prefer OTLP/HTTP on `4318` unless you specifically need gRPC.

**SigNoz Cloud is different:** it uses **port `443` for both** OTLP/gRPC and OTLP/HTTP, so the endpoint is always `https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443`. Do not use `4317` or `4318` for SigNoz Cloud.
</Admonition>

</details>

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### Enable Collector diagnostic logs
</ToggleHeading>

If the Collector itself is erroring, raise its internal log level to see why:

```yaml:otel-collector-config.yaml
service:
  telemetry:
    logs:
      level: DEBUG
```

</details>

## Errors you might see

First turn on your diagnostic logs ([Step 2](#step-2-is-your-telemetry-being-exported)): for a direct SDK export they appear in your application's own console, and with a Collector, in the Collector's logs. Then find the entry below that matches what you see. (A wrong region is the exception, with no error at all: exports succeed and the only sign is an empty view in the SigNoz UI.)

### 1. `401`, `403`, or `Unauthenticated`

Your ingestion key is missing, wrong, expired, or revoked, so the endpoint rejects the request before it accepts any data. Set the key correctly: `signoz-ingestion-key=<your-ingestion-key>` in the OTLP headers for a direct export, or under the `otlphttp` exporter `headers` for a Collector. Then confirm it has not expired or been revoked. See [ingestion keys](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/keys/).

### 2. `404 Not Found`

The URL is wrong. The usual causes are a trailing slash (`.../v1/logs/` instead of `.../v1/logs`), the wrong signal path, or using `4317`/`4318` instead of `443` for SigNoz Cloud. Use exactly `https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443` for SigNoz Cloud, or `http://<signoz-host>:4318` for self-hosted. See [how to find your ingestion URL](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/overview/#endpoint).

### 3. `connection refused`, a timeout, or `no such host`

Nothing is listening at the address you are exporting to. If you export directly, the endpoint host or port is wrong, or a firewall is blocking outbound `443`. If you use a Collector, it is not running or not reachable: confirm it is listening (`netstat -tuln | grep -E '4317|4318'`), that your app's `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` points at the right host (in Kubernetes, the Service DNS name such as `http://otel-collector.observability.svc.cluster.local:4318`), and that network policies and security groups allow the connection.

### 4. Exports succeed, but nothing appears in SigNoz

There is no error in your logs, yet the UI stays empty. This is almost always a wrong region: data sent to a region that does not match your account never shows up, even with a valid-looking key. Confirm your `<region>` under **Settings → Ingestion** or the [region and endpoint table](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/overview/#endpoint). If the region is correct, widen the time range in the UI, since recently sent data can fall outside a narrow window.

### 5. Data arrives, then drops off or goes missing

If telemetry showed up and then stopped, or only part of it arrives, your ingestion key may have hit its rate or volume limit and SigNoz is throttling. Review [ingestion key limits](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/keys/), and set up [meter alerts](https://signoz.io/docs/cost-meter/alerts/) to catch this before data drops.

For the complete list of ingestion HTTP and gRPC status codes with root causes and resolutions, see the [Ingestion Troubleshooting guide](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/troubleshooting/troubleshooting/).

## Other things that can silently drop data

These SigNoz Cloud limits drop data without an obvious instrumentation error: exports are rejected or throttled after they leave your app, so nothing looks wrong on your side.

- **Payload too large.** Payloads must be under the **16 MB** maximum, or they are rejected (HTTP `413`). Reduce batch sizes or export intervals.
- **Timeouts on large or slow exports.** Increase the export timeout with `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_TIMEOUT` (in milliseconds, for example `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_TIMEOUT=30000`) and enable gzip with `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_COMPRESSION=gzip`.

See [ingestion considerations](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/overview/#important-considerations) for details.

## How your telemetry reaches SigNoz

Background for the steps above. SigNoz ingests data **only in OTLP** (over gRPC or HTTP); the port depends on where you send it:

- **SigNoz Cloud** accepts OTLP/gRPC and OTLP/HTTP on port `443`, so the endpoint is always `https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443`, sent with the `signoz-ingestion-key` header. Find your region and key under **Settings → Ingestion**.
- **Self-hosted SigNoz and OpenTelemetry Collectors** use the default ports `4317` (gRPC) and `4318` (HTTP), and need no ingestion key by default.

Anything that is not OTLP (Prometheus scrape output, StatsD packets, Jaeger/Zipkin spans) must be converted to OTLP by an OpenTelemetry Collector first.

Instrumenting your app always comes first: your instrumentation (the SDK, a zero-code agent, or libraries you add) is what produces the telemetry. A Collector is optional, added later to batch, enrich, or scrape non-OTLP sources. The only thing your choice changes on the app side is the export endpoint, `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT`:

- **Direct to SigNoz:** point it at `https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443`.
- **Through a Collector:** point it at the Collector (for example `http://<collector-host>:4318`); the Collector then holds the SigNoz endpoint and ingestion key. It can be a [K8s-Infra chart](https://signoz.io/docs/opentelemetry-collection-agents/k8s/k8s-infra/overview/), the [OpenTelemetry Operator](https://signoz.io/docs/opentelemetry-collection-agents/k8s/otel-operator/overview/), a [host binary](https://signoz.io/docs/opentelemetry-collection-agents/vm/overview/), or a [Docker container](https://signoz.io/docs/opentelemetry-collection-agents/docker/overview/).
