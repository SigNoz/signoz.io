## Overview

If your traces, logs, or metrics are not appearing in SigNoz, the problem is almost always in one of three places along the path from your application to SigNoz. This guide walks that path in order so you can isolate the failure quickly instead of guessing.

Work through the three steps below in sequence. Each step tells you what to check, what a healthy result looks like, and where to go next based on what you find.

1. [Is your application generating telemetry?](#step-1-is-your-application-generating-telemetry) Confirm the data exists before worrying about where it goes.
2. [Is your telemetry being exported?](#step-2-is-your-telemetry-being-exported) Enable diagnostic logging and read the exporter errors.
3. [Is your telemetry reaching SigNoz?](#step-3-is-your-telemetry-reaching-signoz) Verify connectivity and the ingestion endpoint or Collector.

Most "no data" issues are a wrong ingestion endpoint, a wrong region, or an incorrect ingestion key. If you already know your instrumentation works, jump straight to [Common errors and fixes](#common-errors-and-fixes).

## Quick debug flowchart

Use this decision tree to isolate the failure fast. It works the same for traces, logs, and metrics: substitute your signal for `<SIGNAL>` (`TRACES`, `LOGS`, or `METRICS`).

```text
START: <signal> not showing up in SigNoz
│
├─ 1. Is the app generating telemetry?
│      Set OTEL_<SIGNAL>_EXPORTER=console, restart, exercise the code path.
│      ├─ Nothing in stdout ──► Instrumentation problem.
│      │                        Check SDK init, runtime version, library support.
│      └─ You see output ─────► go to 2
│
├─ 2. Is your app exporting it?  (SDK side, in your application)
│      Turn on the SDK's own logging: OTEL_LOG_LEVEL=debug (or language
│      equivalent), then read the export errors it prints.
│      ├─ Export error ───────► fix in this order: KEY ▸ ENDPOINT ▸ REGION
│      │                        (see Common errors and fixes)
│      └─ Exports look clean ─► go to 3
│
└─ 3. Is it reaching SigNoz?  (network / Collector side)
       ├─ Direct to Cloud ──► curl -v https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443
       │                      Fails       => firewall / network policy
       │                      Connects,   => wrong key / endpoint / region
       │                      still no data
       └─ Via Collector ────► add the Collector's 'debug' exporter (a pipeline
                              component, not the SDK log level above) to see what
                              the Collector receives, then check both hops:
                              netstat -tuln | grep -E '4317|4318'  (app → Collector)
                              service.telemetry.logs.level: DEBUG  (Collector → SigNoz)
```

## Signal-specific notes

Each signal takes a slightly different path to SigNoz. Below is a one-line summary per signal; expand the toggle for common sources and what to check when that specific signal is missing. For the full picture of the paths and ports, see [How your telemetry reaches SigNoz](#how-your-telemetry-reaches-signoz) at the end of this page.

### Traces

Traces reach SigNoz from the OpenTelemetry SDK's OTLP exporter, either directly or through a Collector.

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### More on traces
</ToggleHeading>

- **Common sources:** OpenTelemetry SDK; Jaeger/Zipkin clients. Spans from **Jaeger or Zipkin clients** must pass through a Collector with the matching receiver, which converts them to OTLP.
- If some spans are missing or you see gaps in a trace, the instrumentation is likely producing data but some spans are dropped or uninstrumented. See [Traces Management FAQs](https://signoz.io/docs/traces-management/troubleshooting/faqs/).
- SigNoz builds the **Services** list in APM by grouping incoming spans on their `service.name` resource attribute. So if your traces are arriving but a service is missing from the Services tab, it usually means `service.name` is unset or not unique — set a unique `service.name` on that service so its spans are attributed to it.

</details>

### Logs

Logs reach SigNoz from the OpenTelemetry SDK or log appenders, from files on disk read by the Collector's `filelog` receiver, or from log shippers — over OTLP, directly or through a Collector.

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### More on logs
</ToggleHeading>

**Common sources:** OpenTelemetry SDK / log appenders; files on disk; log shippers (FluentBit, Fluentd, Logstash, Vector, Telegraf). Identify your path first:

- **Log shippers (FluentBit, Fluentd, Logstash, Vector, Telegraf) or the Collector.** Check that the receivers and exporters are wired into the pipeline correctly, that the exporter endpoint and ingestion key are set, and read the Collector or shipper logs for errors.
- **Files on disk.** The Collector's `filelog` receiver reads log files. This is the standard path for Kubernetes and Docker. Confirm the receiver's include paths match your log file locations.
- **SDK or log-appender bridge.** Run the [console exporter check](#step-1-is-your-application-generating-telemetry) with `OTEL_LOGS_EXPORTER=console` to confirm the SDK emits logs, then follow Steps 2 and 3.

To correlate logs with traces, make sure each log line carries `trace_id` and `span_id` and parse them with the [trace parser pipeline](https://signoz.io/docs/logs-pipelines/guides/trace/). More in [Logs Management FAQs](https://signoz.io/docs/logs-management/troubleshooting/faqs/).

</details>

### Metrics

Metrics reach SigNoz from the OpenTelemetry SDK, or from Prometheus, StatsD, and host or Kubernetes sources collected by the Collector's receivers — exported as OTLP.

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### More on metrics
</ToggleHeading>

- **OpenTelemetry SDK metrics** export OTLP directly or through a Collector.
- **Prometheus metrics** are collected by the Collector's `prometheus` receiver, which scrapes your `/metrics` endpoints.
- **StatsD** metrics are received by the Collector's `statsd` receiver (application pushes UDP packets to the listener), then exported as OTLP.
- **Host and Kubernetes metrics** come from the `hostmetrics` receiver and the [K8s-Infra chart](https://signoz.io/docs/opentelemetry-collection-agents/k8s/k8s-infra/overview/).
- For any Collector receiver, confirm the receivers and exporters are added to the pipeline correctly, the exporter endpoint and key are set, and check the Collector logs for errors.
- If a specific metric is missing, a common cause is a temporality mismatch (delta versus cumulative) for that metric. See [Metrics Management FAQs](https://signoz.io/docs/metrics-management/troubleshooting/faqs/).

</details>

## Step 1: Is your application generating telemetry?

Before checking the network, confirm the telemetry actually exists. The most reliable way is to route the data to your console so you can see it printed locally.

### Using the SDK console exporter

For SDK and auto-instrumented applications, set the exporter to `console`. These are standard OpenTelemetry SDK environment variables and work across languages that support auto-instrumentation:

```bash
export OTEL_TRACES_EXPORTER=console
export OTEL_METRICS_EXPORTER=console
export OTEL_LOGS_EXPORTER=console
```

To keep exporting to SigNoz **and** print to the console at the same time, use a comma-separated list:

```bash
export OTEL_TRACES_EXPORTER=otlp,console
```

Restart your application and exercise the code path you expect to produce data (send a request, run the job, emit a log).

- **You see spans, metrics, or logs printed in stdout.** Your instrumentation works. The data exists, so the problem is in export or connectivity. Continue to [Step 2](#step-2-is-your-telemetry-being-exported).
- **You see nothing printed.** Your application is not producing telemetry. This is an instrumentation problem, not a networking one. See [When no data is generated](#when-no-data-is-generated) below.

<Admonition type="tip">
For a Collector-based setup, do the equivalent check at the Collector using the [debug exporter](#verify-data-inside-the-collector) in Step 3. That tells you whether data is reaching the Collector at all.
</Admonition>

### When no data is generated

If nothing is printed to the console, the instrumentation is not active or is not supported for your setup. Check the following:

- **The instrumentation is actually initialized.** Auto-instrumentation must be loaded before your app code runs (for example, the Java agent via `-javaagent`, `opentelemetry-instrument` for Python, or requiring the SDK first in Node.js). Follow the setup steps in your language's [instrumentation guide](https://signoz.io/docs/instrumentation/overview/).
- **Your runtime version is supported.** Very old runtimes (for example, .NET Framework 4.5, Node.js 14, Python 3.1) may be outside the supported range for the OpenTelemetry SDK. Check the requirements in your language's guide and in the <a href="https://opentelemetry.io/docs/languages/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">OpenTelemetry language documentation</a>.
- **Your third-party library version is supported.** Auto-instrumentation hooks into specific versions of frameworks and clients. If a library is too new or too old, its spans may not be produced. See the supported libraries lists in the <a href="https://opentelemetry.io/docs/languages/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">OpenTelemetry docs</a>.
- **The code path is exercised.** Confirm the request or job you expect to trace actually ran while instrumentation was active.

## Step 2: Is your telemetry being exported?

If the data exists but is not reaching SigNoz, the exporter is either failing or misconfigured. Turn on the SDK's diagnostic logging to see the exporter's own error messages.

<Tabs entityName="language">
<TabItem value="nodejs" label="Node.js" default>

Set the log level to `debug`:

```bash
export OTEL_LOG_LEVEL=debug
```

The SDK prints exporter activity and any connection or authentication errors to the console.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="java" label="Java">

Enable the Java agent's debug logging:

```bash
export OTEL_JAVAAGENT_DEBUG=true
# or as a JVM flag: -Dotel.javaagent.debug=true
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

Set the SDK log level and route internal logs to the console. Refer to the <a href="https://opentelemetry.io/docs/languages/python/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">OpenTelemetry Python docs</a> for enabling internal diagnostic logging, then re-run your app and read the exporter output.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="other" label="Other languages">

Enable the SDK's internal (self-diagnostic) logging as described in your language's <a href="https://opentelemetry.io/docs/languages/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">OpenTelemetry documentation</a>, then re-run your application and read the exporter output.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

With diagnostic logging on, restart the app and look at the output:

- **You see an export error.** The exporter is trying and failing. Match the error against [Common errors and fixes](#common-errors-and-fixes) to correct the endpoint, region, or key.
- **You see no errors and exports appear to succeed.** The data is leaving your application cleanly. Continue to [Step 3](#step-3-is-your-telemetry-reaching-signoz).

## Step 3: Is your telemetry reaching SigNoz?

How you verify this depends on your topology.

### If you export directly to SigNoz Cloud

Test that the ingestion endpoint is reachable from the machine running your application:

```bash
curl -v https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443
```

**Verify these values:**

- `<region>` is the region shown under **Settings → Ingestion** in the SigNoz UI. See the [ingestion overview](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/overview/).

If the connection fails, a firewall or network policy is blocking outbound access to the endpoint. If it connects but data still does not appear, the problem is the endpoint, region, or key, covered in [Common errors and fixes](#common-errors-and-fixes). For a full catalog of ingestion status codes (400, 401, 404, and so on) and their fixes, see the [Ingestion Troubleshooting guide](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/troubleshooting/troubleshooting/).

### If you use an OpenTelemetry Collector

There are two links to verify: application → Collector, and Collector → SigNoz.

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### Verify data inside the Collector
</ToggleHeading>

This `debug` exporter is a Collector pipeline component, different from the SDK's `OTEL_LOG_LEVEL=debug` in [Step 2](#step-2-is-your-telemetry-being-exported): Step 2 shows whether your *application* is exporting, while this shows what the *Collector* actually receives. Add it to the relevant pipeline in your existing `otel-collector-config.yaml` (do not replace the whole file):

```yaml:otel-collector-config.yaml
receivers:
  otlp:                                                # data coming IN from your app
    protocols:
      grpc:
        endpoint: 0.0.0.0:4317                         # OTLP/gRPC — your app sends here
      http:
        endpoint: 0.0.0.0:4318                         # OTLP/HTTP — your app sends here

exporters:
  debug:
    verbosity: detailed
  otlphttp:                                            # data going OUT to SigNoz
    endpoint: https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443 # SigNoz Cloud (port 443, not 4317/4318)
    headers:
      signoz-ingestion-key: <your-ingestion-key>

service:
  pipelines:
    traces:
      receivers: [otlp]
      processors: [batch]
      exporters: [debug, otlphttp]
    logs:
      receivers: [otlp]
      processors: [batch]
      exporters: [debug, otlphttp]
    metrics:
      receivers: [otlp]
      processors: [batch]
      exporters: [debug, otlphttp]
```

The Collector has two sides, and they use **different ports** — this is where most people mix things up:

- **Receivers** (`otlp`) are the ports the Collector *listens on* for data coming from your application: `4317` for OTLP/gRPC and `4318` for OTLP/HTTP. Point your app's `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` at these (for example `http://<collector-host>:4318`).
- **Exporters** (`otlphttp`) are where the Collector *sends* data onward. For SigNoz Cloud the endpoint is `https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443` (port `443`, **not** `4317`/`4318`); for self-hosted SigNoz it is `http://<signoz-host>:4318`.

So `443` only appears on the exporter side (Collector → SigNoz Cloud); `4317`/`4318` only appear on the receiver side (your app → Collector).

**Verify these values:**

- `<region>` and `<your-ingestion-key>` come from **Settings → Ingestion** in the SigNoz UI. See [ingestion keys](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/keys/).

Restart the Collector and check its logs.

- **You see data in the debug output but not in SigNoz.** The Collector receives data but cannot export it. The problem is the `otlphttp` exporter config (endpoint, region, or key). See [Common errors and fixes](#common-errors-and-fixes).
- **You see no data in the debug output.** Data is not reaching the Collector. Check that the application can connect to the Collector, below.

Reference: <a href="https://github.com/open-telemetry/opentelemetry-collector/blob/main/exporter/debugexporter/README.md" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">debug exporter README</a> and the <a href="https://opentelemetry.io/docs/collector/troubleshooting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">Collector troubleshooting guide</a>.

</details>

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### Verify the application can reach the Collector
</ToggleHeading>

Confirm the application points at the Collector:

```bash
echo $OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT
# Expect http://localhost:4318 (OTLP/HTTP) or your Collector host and port
```

Confirm the Collector is listening on the OTLP ports:

```bash
netstat -tuln | grep -E '4317|4318'
```

Ports `4317` and `4318` should be in the `LISTEN` state.

For Kubernetes, verify the Collector Service has endpoints:

```bash
kubectl get svc otel-collector -n observability
kubectl get endpoints otel-collector -n observability
```

<Admonition type="warning">
Watch for port and protocol mismatches when talking to a **Collector or self-hosted SigNoz**: OTLP/gRPC uses port `4317` and OTLP/HTTP uses port `4318`. If your SDK is configured for HTTP but points at `4317` (or the reverse), exports fail silently or with connection errors. Prefer OTLP/HTTP on `4318` unless you specifically need gRPC.

**SigNoz Cloud is different:** it uses **port `443` for both** OTLP/gRPC and OTLP/HTTP, so the endpoint is always `https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443`. Do not use `4317` or `4318` for SigNoz Cloud.
</Admonition>

</details>

<details>
<ToggleHeading>
#### Enable Collector diagnostic logs
</ToggleHeading>

If the Collector itself is erroring, raise its internal log level to see why:

```yaml:otel-collector-config.yaml
service:
  telemetry:
    logs:
      level: DEBUG
```

</details>

## Common errors and fixes

These are the most frequent causes of missing data, in rough order of how often they occur. They apply whether you export directly or through a Collector.

<Tabs entityName="issue">
<TabItem value="endpoint" label="Wrong endpoint or URL" default>

**Symptom:** Connection refused, connection timeout, or no data despite successful instrumentation.

**Fix:**

- Use the exact ingestion endpoint for your setup: `https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443` for SigNoz Cloud, or `http://<signoz-host>:4318` for self-hosted.
- Make sure the protocol matches the port: OTLP/HTTP on `4318`, OTLP/gRPC on `4317`.
- Do not point an application-side SDK at a Collector that is not running, and do not point at SigNoz through a Collector endpoint that does not exist.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="region" label="Wrong region">

**Symptom:** Data is accepted by the exporter but never appears in your account, or you get authentication errors even with a valid key.

**Fix:** The `<region>` in your endpoint must match the region your SigNoz Cloud account is in. A key from the EU region will not authenticate against the US endpoint. Confirm both the region and key under **Settings → Ingestion**.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="key" label="Wrong or expired ingestion key">

**Symptom:** HTTP `401` or `403`, or gRPC `Unauthenticated` or `PermissionDenied`.

**Fix:**

- Confirm the ingestion key is set correctly. For direct SDK export, it goes in the OTLP headers as `signoz-ingestion-key=<your-ingestion-key>`; for the Collector, under the `otlphttp` exporter `headers`.
- Check the key has not expired or been revoked, and that its limits are not blocking your data. See [ingestion keys](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/keys/).

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="trailing-slash" label="Trailing slash / path">

**Symptom:** HTTP `404`.

**Fix:** Remove trailing slashes from the endpoint (for example, use `.../v1/logs`, not `.../v1/logs/`). A trailing slash or an incorrect path segment causes a not-found error at ingestion.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="collector-access" label="Collector not accessible">

**Symptom:** Application logs show connection refused or timeout to the Collector; no data in the Collector debug output.

**Fix:**

- Confirm the Collector is running and listening on `4317`/`4318` (`netstat -tuln | grep -E '4317|4318'`).
- Confirm the application's `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` points at the correct Collector host. In Kubernetes, use the Service DNS name (for example, `http://otel-collector.observability.svc.cluster.local:4318`).
- Check network policies, security groups, and firewalls between the application and the Collector.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

For the complete list of ingestion HTTP and gRPC status codes with root causes and resolutions, see the [Ingestion Troubleshooting guide](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/troubleshooting/troubleshooting/).

### Other things that can silently block data

These SigNoz Cloud limits reject or delay data without an obvious instrumentation error:

- **Payload too large.** Payloads must be under the **16 MB** maximum, or they are rejected (HTTP `413`). Reduce batch sizes or export intervals.
- **Timeouts on large or slow exports.** Increase the export timeout with `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_TIMEOUT` (in milliseconds, for example `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_TIMEOUT=30000`) and enable gzip with `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_COMPRESSION=gzip`.
- **Ingestion key limits.** A key with restrictive limits can throttle your data. Review [ingestion key limits](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/keys/).

See [ingestion considerations](https://signoz.io/docs/ingestion/signoz-cloud/overview/#important-considerations) for details.

## How your telemetry reaches SigNoz

This is background for the steps above: how telemetry gets from your application to SigNoz, and which ports each hop uses.

SigNoz ingests data **only in OTLP** (OpenTelemetry Protocol), over either gRPC or HTTP. The port depends on *where* you send it, not on the protocol:

- **SigNoz Cloud** accepts both OTLP/gRPC and OTLP/HTTP on port `443` (standard HTTPS), so the endpoint is always `https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443`.
- **Self-hosted SigNoz and OpenTelemetry Collectors** use the OpenTelemetry default ports: `4317` for OTLP/gRPC and `4318` for OTLP/HTTP.

Anything that is not OTLP, such as Prometheus scrape output, StatsD packets, or Jaeger/Zipkin spans, must be converted to OTLP by an OpenTelemetry Collector before it reaches SigNoz.

There are two topologies:

- **Direct to SigNoz.** Your application's instrumentation exports OTLP straight to the SigNoz ingestion endpoint.
- **Through a Collector.** Your application (or a third-party agent) sends data to an OpenTelemetry Collector, which then exports OTLP to SigNoz. The Collector may be a [K8s-Infra chart](https://signoz.io/docs/opentelemetry-collection-agents/k8s/k8s-infra/overview/), the [OpenTelemetry Operator](https://signoz.io/docs/opentelemetry-collection-agents/k8s/otel-operator/overview/), a [host binary](https://signoz.io/docs/opentelemetry-collection-agents/vm/overview/), or a [Docker container](https://signoz.io/docs/opentelemetry-collection-agents/docker/overview/).

Whichever topology you use, **instrumenting your application always comes first** — your instrumentation is what produces the telemetry, whether that is the OpenTelemetry SDK, a zero-code auto-instrumentation agent, or instrumentation libraries you add to your code. A Collector is optional and added later as an intermediary (for example, to batch data, enrich it, or scrape non-OTLP sources). Which path you choose decides one setting on the app side: the export endpoint, `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT`.

- **Direct to SigNoz Cloud.** Point your instrumentation at `https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443` and set the `signoz-ingestion-key` header.
- **Through a Collector.** Point your instrumentation at the Collector instead (for example `http://<collector-host>:4318`). The Collector, not the app, then holds the SigNoz Cloud endpoint and ingestion key. Adding a Collector only changes where your instrumentation exports to; it does not change how you instrument the app.

<Admonition type="info">
SigNoz Cloud endpoints use the form `https://ingest.<region>.signoz.cloud:443`. Find your region and ingestion key under **Settings → Ingestion** in the SigNoz UI. For self-hosted SigNoz, the endpoint is your SigNoz host on port `4317` (gRPC) or `4318` (HTTP), and no ingestion key is required by default.
</Admonition>
